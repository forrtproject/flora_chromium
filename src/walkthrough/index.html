ORE tells you what is known about the papers in front of you

Whenever you are reading, ORE looks up the papers on the page and reports what the research record says about them: whether anyone has tried to replicate them, whether they have been retracted or questioned, whether researchers are discussing them on PubPeer, and whether a free copy exists.

It works in three places. The next screens show you each one.

Every result in a search gets its own panel, so you can judge a paper before you open it.

Google Scholar 2 of 9

Judge a result before you open it

ORE adds a panel beside every Google Scholar result. A search returns twenty papers and tells you almost nothing about any of them; the panel puts the evidence next to each one, so you can decide which are worth your time without opening a single tab.

Each row is an action, not just a label — open the DOI, copy the reference, jump to a free copy, read the PubPeer thread, or open the paper's entry in the FLoRA Replication Atlas.

The citation is ready to paste
The quote icon copies the reference in the style you picked in Settings, with its italics intact — so it arrives in Word or Google Docs correctly formatted, and anywhere else as plain text.
Read the status in each row
Rows stay in place while checks run and when nothing is found. Read the status to distinguish an empty result from a check that is still pending or unavailable.

Google Scholar 3 of 9

When the DOI has to be searched for

Google Scholar does not always print a DOI. When none is on the page, ORE searches Crossref and OpenAlex for the paper's title, then narrows the candidates by first author and year. That usually finds the right record — but it is a best match, not a fact read off the page, and the panel says so.

A searched-for DOI is underlined with dots. Hover it and ORE tells you it was matched by search. Everything below that row behaves exactly as before.

Check a dotted DOI before you cite it
Papers with near-identical titles do exist, and a wrong match means the replication counts and retraction status below belong to a different paper. Open the DOI once and confirm it is the paper you meant.

Journal & publisher pages 4 of 9

On an article page, two things appear

A pill is placed next to the article's own DOI, and next to the references it cites. It is deliberately small: it marks a paper without getting between you and what you are reading, and it carries every signal at once so a page of forty references does not become forty badges.

A tab appears on the right edge of the window. It opens the Meta Report — everything ORE found about this article and its references, in one place. Drag it up or down if it sits over something you need.

Pills identify recognised references
Each recognised reference gets a pill. When its DOI is already linked on the page, ORE places the pill after that link; otherwise it adds one beside the reference.
Sites you can switch off
If ORE gets in the way on a particular site, block that domain in Settings and it will stay out of it entirely.

Reading the pill 5 of 9

The pill is a summary; the panel is the detail

When closed, the pill answers one question per icon: is there a DOI, a free copy, a discussion, a replication. A dimmed icon is not a guarantee that a check succeeded. Open the panel for the current status. Icons stay in the same order; counts can make the pill wider.

Point at it and the full panel opens, turning each icon into a row you can act on. Click to keep it open.

Chain link — which paper this is
Every other signal depends on it. A dotted underline means ORE found the DOI by searching, so check it. The row copies the DOI or the reference, or opens doi.org.
Padlock — a free copy exists
Lit when Unpaywall knows of a legal one. The row opens the best copy; the chevron lists the rest, for when it is the wrong version or a dead link.
PubPeer mark — the discussion after publication
Where researchers raise failed reanalyses, duplicated images, statistics that do not add up. The row shows the comment count and opens the thread.
Dot — replications, or a warning
A count of replications and reproductions, opening the paper's Atlas entry. A red or amber ! replaces it for a retraction or concern — next screen.

Withdrawn & questioned work 6 of 9

Retractions and concerns are not the same thing

Both come from the Retraction Watch database, and ORE keeps them visibly apart because they mean different things. A retraction is a settled matter: the paper has been withdrawn from the record. An expression of concern is unsettled: the journal has flagged a problem that has not yet been resolved, and the paper still stands.

Either one replaces the replication count in the pill and is called out in the Meta Report, on the article itself and on any reference it cites. Both link to the notice, so you can read what was actually said.

Checked offline, refreshed weekly
The retraction list ships with the extension and updates in the background, so a DOI is checked without asking any server what you are reading.
Retracted Withdrawn from the record. The pill's last icon turns red.
Expression of concern Flagged but unresolved. An amber warning triangle, not a red stop.
Journal & publisher pages 7 of 9

The Meta Report: the whole picture, one panel

Open the side tab and ORE collects everything it found into a single report: every replication and reproduction on record with its outcome, any retraction or expression of concern, which of the paper's references have their own entry in the FLoRA Replication Atlas or a PubPeer thread, and the PubPeer discussion of the article itself. If the paper you are reading is itself a replication, the report shows the original study it set out to replicate instead.

What the FLoRA Replication Atlas is
FORRT's open database of replication attempts in the social and behavioural sciences. Volunteers read the published literature and record, for each original finding, which studies tried to replicate it and what they found — with a quote from the replication itself saying so. It is the source of every replication count ORE shows, and clicking one opens that paper's entry, so you can read the evidence rather than take the number on trust.
Save it
The download button opens the report as a clean printable page — print to PDF to keep it with your notes, or hand it to a co-author.
Share it
The share button copies a link that opens the report as a web page, for colleagues who do not have ORE. The report travels inside the link itself — nothing is uploaded, and no server ever sees what you were reading.

Google Sheets 8 of 9

Screen a whole spreadsheet of DOIs

A screening sheet, a reading list, a set of candidate studies for a meta-analysis — open it in Google Sheets and ORE reads every DOI in it, including the rows you have not scrolled to, and reports back in a popup.

Retracted papers come first. Finding a retraction in a list you were about to analyse is the single most useful thing ORE can tell you here, so the popup opens for a retraction or an expression of concern even when nothing in the sheet has replication data at all — each one named, with a link to the notice.

One tab at a time
Switching sheet tabs starts a fresh scan, and each tab remembers whether you dismissed its popup — so silencing one does not silence the rest.
Dismiss or snooze
Dismiss closes the popup for this tab; the clock snoozes it for ten minutes, for when you are mid-edit and will want it back shortly.

That's all of it

ORE checks the papers on supported pages as you browse. While it works, a small note sits in the bottom corner of the page and then disappears — that is the whole of its footprint until it has something to tell you.

Set your citation style

Pick the style the copy button uses — APA, Vancouver, BibTeX and eight more.

Add your email

Unpaywall asks for a contact address before it will report free copies. ORE also sends it to Crossref, OpenAlex and NCBI for identifier and citation lookups. You can remove it in Settings.

Block a site

Any domain where you would rather not see ORE at all can be switched off.
